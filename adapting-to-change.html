Change can take time to adjust to

Adapting does not mean liking the change. It means seeing what is true clearly enough to choose a useful response.

This is a self-guided practice for manageable changes. Use it only when you are safe enough to pause and reflect.

Safety Check

This practice is for manageable changes. Do not use it to delay protective action or urgent help.

If you or someone else is in immediate danger, call 911, text or call 988, or go to the nearest emergency room.

Before you begin

How clear does this change feel right now? Hover or focus on a number for the meaning.
How much energy are you spending wishing this change were different? Hover or focus on a number for the meaning.

What changed?

Choose the specific kind of change you are trying to adapt to right now. This might be something that happened, something that did not happen, or something that feels different than it used to.

Examples

My schedule changed. A relationship feels different. I have a new responsibility. Something I expected did not happen. My body, energy, or focus is different.

Choose the closest match

Pick one. If none fit, choose Something else and write the change in one sentence.

Predictability and control
Roles, relationships, and support
How I am responding

Describe the change with guidance

Choose a starter statement if one fits. You can edit the words so they match your real situation.

Starter statements

Separate what changed from what your mind is adding

Facts are what happened. Meaning is what you fear, wish, predict, or believe the change means.

Does the first box describe what changed, without adding guesses, judgments, or worst-case meanings?
The first box should say only what someone could verify happened. Put fears, predictions, judgments, or guesses in the second box.

Choose one workable step

Now that the change is clearer, choose what you can influence next.

What can you influence next?
What exactly will you do?
Could you begin this action in under five minutes?

Guided adaptation review

Use the fact statement as the foundation for the rest of the practice.

How are your mind or body responding?
What feelings are present?
What cannot change right now?
What do you not know yet?
Choose a reality statement
Rehearse your preparation: when this feels difficult again, what will you do first?

After you practice

How clear does this change feel now? Hover or focus on a number for the meaning.
How much energy are you spending wishing this change were different now? Hover or focus on a number for the meaning.

You practiced seeing what changed clearly, making room for your reaction, and adjusting one workable step.
