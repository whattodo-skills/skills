Listen to Understand

Understand first. Respond second.

Pause your own response, clarify what the other person means, reflect it accurately, and respond only after checking your understanding. Understanding does not require agreement.

Validation communicates that part of the person’s experience is understandable. It does not confirm every interpretation as fact.

Optional model demonstration

Morgan: “You changed the meeting agenda without asking me. I had spent most of the morning preparing for the original discussion. When I saw the new agenda, it felt like my work did not matter.”

  1. Which part of the change affected you most?
  2. The main issue was not only that the agenda changed. It was that you were not included or given time to adjust after doing the preparation.
  3. Did I get that right?
  4. I can understand why that was frustrating after you had put in the work.
  5. Would it help to talk more, clarify next steps, or hear my perspective?
