Name The Emotion

Emotions are information. They are messengers that tell you something may matter, need attention, feel threatened, feel meaningful, or need care.

A feeling is how an emotion shows up in your lived experience, such as body sensations, urges, thoughts, facial expressions, or the mood you notice. An emotion is the message pattern underneath the feeling, such as anger, fear, sadness, joy, shame, surprise, or calm.

In this exercise, you are not trying to change, fix, or judge what you feel. You are practising how to listen to the message, name it more clearly, and decide what to do next.

Choose an ordinary, low-to-moderate emotional experience rather than a traumatic or highly distressing event. You may pass, stop, or keep your responses private.
How this exercise works
  • Notice what is happening internally.
  • Learn the difference between an emotion and the way it feels in your body and mind.
  • Name one emotion more precisely.
  • Check whether another emotion is also present.
  • Choose one small next action.

1. Notice

Take one slow breath and briefly notice what is happening internally.

What am I feeling right now, or what did I feel during a recent everyday situation?

You might recall a meeting, conversation, delay, mistake, decision, success, or unexpected event.
