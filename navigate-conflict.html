Navigate Conflict

Slow down one disagreement, learn from it, and prepare a clear response.

Use conflict as information.

Conflict is not automatically a sign that a relationship is failing. When it is safe enough to engage, disagreement can show you what you need, what you assume, what matters to the other person, and how the two of you handle difference.

How you respond also teaches the other person how to relate to you—what you will listen to, what you will repair, what you will not participate in, and how you expect disagreement to be handled.

Desired outcome: Leave with one clearer understanding, one usable response, one targeted improvement, and one practical next step.
Curiosity is not the same as mutuality. Understanding another perspective does not require treating both positions as equally valid. Some conflicts call for a boundary, support, or distance rather than better communication.
