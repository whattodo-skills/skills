Act Opposite: Choose Your Next Move

Name the urge. Check it. Choose what helps.

Every emotion pulls you toward an action. Sometimes that action helps; other times it makes the emotion or situation harder. Use this practice to choose one effective next move.

1. Name the urge

You are not trying to prove that your emotion is wrong. You are deciding what action will help.
What emotion are you feeling?
What is the emotion urging you to do?
Intensity now (optional)

2. Check the urge

Is there a problem you can address or repair?
Does the emotion—including its intensity—fit the facts?
Would following the urge help the situation and your longer-term goal?
Need an example?

Emotional urge

Jordan wants to call in sick and avoid a prepared meeting update.

Effective action

Jordan attends, gives the prepared update, and remains engaged.

Why: Some nervousness fits, but avoiding would strengthen the pattern. Jordan can sit upright, speak steadily, and answer one question at a time.
