Opposite Action Decision Lab

Decide when Opposite Action is appropriate.

Separate facts from assumptions, check whether following the emotional urge would be effective, and build a specific, whole-body action that moves you toward a better response.

Ready to practice?

Choose a recent, manageable situation or use a fictional scenario. The goal is to decide whether following the emotional urge would help, then build a more effective response when it would not.

Your task is to choose an effective response—not to prove that an emotion is wrong.

Warm up: emotion and urge

Notice what an emotion commonly pulls a person to do. An urge can offer short-term relief and still create a longer-term cost.

Check whether Opposite Action fits

Use Opposite Action when the emotion does not fit the facts, its intensity is greater than the facts support, or following its urge would be ineffective.

Worked example: Jordan’s meeting

Jordan feels fear before giving a prepared update at a team meeting and wants to call in sick.

Try two scenarios

Complete one Opposite Action example and one example where another route may fit better. The likely route stays hidden until you submit.
