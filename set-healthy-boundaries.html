Set Healthy Boundaries

Prepare one clear limit and a response you can realistically carry out.

Practice one complete boundary cycle.

Use this skill when you need to protect your time, privacy, workload, money, personal space, or participation in a conversation or relationship.

Performance outcome: Given a real, past, or fictional low-risk situation, you will distinguish a boundary from a request or control attempt; prepare a statement with an observable condition, clear limit, and realistic action within your control; rehearse it twice; and respond to one likely challenge.
Three useful formats

Simple limit: “I am not available for that.”
Limit plus alternative: “I cannot meet tonight. I can meet Thursday.”
Conditional boundary: “If the yelling continues, I will end the call.”
Choose an appropriate situation. Use a fictional or low-risk example if direct communication could expose you to violence, coercion, stalking, abuse, discrimination, credible retaliation, or danger. A clearer boundary does not make an unsafe situation safe. Do not enter names or identifying details. You do not need to justify or negotiate a refusal of unwanted physical contact.

Estimated time: 10–15 minutes. Personal boundary text is not included in Skills Stack saving or analytics.

Diagnose the difference

Request, boundary, or control?

“You are not allowed to call me after 9:00.”

Keep the action with the speaker. Example: I do not answer non-emergency calls after 9:00.

Protection or punishment?

“If you disagree with me in front of the team again, I will make sure you are removed from the project.”

Example: If you disagree with my proposal, please raise the concern directly. If the discussion becomes personal, I will pause and ask that we return to the work issue.
