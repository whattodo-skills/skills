Set Healthy Boundaries

Identify a limit, communicate it clearly, and decide what you will do if the situation continues.

Protect what matters without trying to control another person.

Use this skill when a repeated behavior is affecting your time, energy, responsibilities, values, or ability to participate in a relationship or situation.

By the end, you will be able to:
  1. Distinguish a request, boundary, and control attempt.
  2. Write a boundary using observable facts, a clear personal limit, and an action within your control.
  3. Deliver it briefly and respectfully.
  4. Prepare a realistic follow-through plan.
The boundary formula
When ______ happens, it affects me by ______.
I am willing to ______.
I am not willing to ______.
If it continues, I will ______.
Choose an appropriate situation. For a first practice, use a fictional or low- to moderate-risk example. Directly communicating a boundary may not be the right first move when there is coercion, stalking, credible retaliation, violence, or a serious power imbalance. A valid boundary does not require direct confrontation.
