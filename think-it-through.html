Show an example

Situation: My manager gave me brief critical feedback, and I want to resign immediately.

Facts: My manager identified two parts of my work that need revision.

Assumption: My manager thinks I am incompetent and wants me gone.

Other possibilities: The work may simply need revision. The message may have been brief because my manager was busy.

Two options: Ask what successful revision would look like. Wait until tomorrow before deciding how to respond.

Smallest effective next step: Draft two specific questions about the feedback.

This instructional example is never saved as your personal practice.

Step 1 of 5

1. Do I need to act now?

Choose one
Does this require emergency or professional support?
If emergency or professional support is needed, use that support now. You do not need to complete this exercise first.
Help me decide
  • Is there a real deadline?
  • Is anyone in danger?
  • Will waiting make the situation worse?
  • Is the urge to act mainly an attempt to escape discomfort?

Step 2 of 5

2. What are the facts, and what am I assuming?

Include what was directly seen, heard, written, received, documented, or confirmed.

Include what you think the event means, what you predict will happen, or what you believe another person intended.

Check my thinking
  • Could a message, document, recording, or witness confirm this?
  • Am I describing another person’s intention?
  • Am I making a prediction?
  • Am I treating a feeling as proof?

3. What else could be true?

These explanations do not need to be positive. They only need to be realistic and consistent with what is known.

Need a deeper review?

You have thought it through enough for this practice when you have:

  • Identified the known facts
  • Noticed what you are assuming
  • Considered two realistic explanations
  • Compared two realistic responses
  • Chosen one proportionate next step

You do not need certainty before acting.
