Choose how to begin

This practice helps you look at a manageable emotional moment without blaming yourself or approving of what happened.

You can pause without committing to any future action. Understanding a trigger is information, not a requirement to forgive, re-engage, or act immediately.

Ground first

Before examining the trigger, orient to the room you are in.

Brief sensory grounding:
Name 5 things you can see.
Name 4 things you can feel or touch.
Name 3 things you can hear.
Take one slow breath and notice that you are here now.

You can pause here

Stopping now is allowed. Pausing does not commit you to any future action, explanation, conversation, or decision.

1. Choose an emotional moment

Choose one recent moment that feels manageable enough to examine.

The emotion I noticed was:
Its intensity was:

2. Find the starting point

What happened just before I noticed the emotion?

Describe what you directly observed through your senses, including an internal sensation, thought, image, or memory.

Examples: My friend read my message but did not respond. My supervisor asked me to revise part of my work. I noticed tightness in my chest. I remembered something embarrassing. I pictured an upcoming appointment.

Statements like “They do not care” or “Something terrible will happen” are interpretations. Put those in the next step.

3. Identify the trigger type

What kind of trigger does this seem closest to?

4. Separate the event from the story

The interpretation may be accurate, partly accurate, or inaccurate. The event and its meaning are not automatically the same.

5. Notice vulnerability factors

Before this happened, what conditions may have made the emotion hit harder?

Common vulnerability factors
Vulnerability does not mean your emotion is wrong. It helps explain why the trigger may have affected you more strongly at that moment.

7. Check understanding

You may find that several triggers were working together. You do not have to force one external event to be the only cause.

Example: Hearing the way our elected officials talk to us and each other. Example: Our elected officials have forgotten they work for us—for you and me—and I am done accepting it. I am going to make sure everyone I know is registered to vote and encourage them to do the same.

8. Choose the next skill

Choosing a skill does not automatically redirect you or commit you to an action. It only helps you name what may help next.

Interpretation may not fit the facts → Check the Facts
Facts are the problem → Problem Solving
Body is highly activated → Body-based regulation or distress tolerance
Event cannot be changed → Radical Acceptance or Turning the Mind
Emotion creates an ineffective urge → Opposite Action
